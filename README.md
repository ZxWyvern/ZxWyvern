I make games that mess with people's heads that's the honest version. Somewhere along the way I got obsessed with psychological horror, not the jump-scare kind, but the slow kind: the kind that makes a player doubt what they just saw, second-guess a sound, or feel watched in a room they know is empty. Fear built out of pacing, silence, and small wrongness rather than loud monsters.

I'm a Game Programmer Lead and software engineering student, and most of my technical work exists to serve that obsession systems for tension, dread, and the quiet manipulation of what a player believes is real. I build in Unity 6, and the tech is always in service of the feeling, never the other way around.

```
Focus areas:  Psychological Horror Design · Player Perception · Atmosphere Systems · Unity 6
```

## What I'm Chasing

I'm interested in the mechanics of fear itself how unreliable narration, sound design, subtle AI behavior, and environmental storytelling can be combined to manipulate a player's sense of safety without them noticing it's happening. Things like:

- Enemies or systems that react to *how* a player plays, not just where they stand
- Audio and lighting that erode trust in the game world over time
- Save systems and world states that make the player question their own memory of events
- Small, deniable details a player isn't sure they actually saw
